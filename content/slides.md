# Using Claude at a Nonprofit

One hour. Four habits.

Notes:
Welcome people as they come in. Introduce yourself in one sentence, and say you're not here to
sell anything.

---

## Today: four habits

1. Ask well
2. Check before using
3. Protect clients
4. Know when not to use it

Notes:
"This isn't about becoming an expert. It's four habits you can use tomorrow."

---

## Quick quiz

No names. Not graded. 3 minutes.

We'll take it again at the end.

Notes:
Hand out the quiz. Collect face down at minute 5.

---

## What Claude is

- It writes by predicting likely words
- That's why it sounds fluent
- And why it can sound sure when it's wrong

Notes:
Keep this short. The point: fluent is not the same as correct.

---

## What Claude doesn't know

- This month's rules (it learned from text up to a date)
- Your organization
- Your clients, and it shouldn't

Notes:
"Unless you tell it. And for clients, you shouldn't tell it. We'll practice that."

---

## What it's good at

- First drafts
- Rewording, plainer language
- Summaries of long documents
- Ideas

Not a source of facts.

Notes:
Ask: "Who's used it already? For what?" Two or three answers, then move on.

---

## Exercise 1: Ask well

Handout 1 · pairs · 12 minutes

Round 1: a short request. Round 2: a better one. Then compare.

Notes:
Keep time: round 1 until minute 15, round 2 until 19, compare until 24.

---

## A good request says

- **Who** it's for
- **How long**
- **What facts** to use: "only the numbers in this report"

Then you still check every number.

Notes:
If Claude left [X] blanks, ask where people would get the numbers to fill them.

---

## Exercise 2: Check before using

Handout 2 · pairs · 12 minutes

What's the SNAP income limit for a family of four?

Notes:
Leave 5 minutes for step 3, the Tennessee conflict.

---

## Checking means

- Find the official source
- Check what dates it's for
- When good sources disagree: don't guess. Apply anyway, and call the agency or **211**

Notes:
"Claude can be out of date. So can a website. The official source and its dates settle it."

---

## Exercise 3: Protect clients

Handout 3 · pairs · 10 minutes

**Don't paste the emails into Claude.**

Notes:
Say this out loud even though the emails are made up. We're practicing the habit.

---

## Ask without private details

- Describe the problem, not the person
- Use "Client A" if you need a name
- Read it again before you press Enter
- Add real details later, in your own systems

Notes:
Hand out the privacy sheet now.

---

## Exercise 4: When not to use it

Handout 4 · groups of 3 or 4 · 9 minutes

Good use · Use with a check · Don't use

Notes:
Five minutes to sort, four to discuss the card each group argued about most.

---

## Where the line is

- Drafts and ideas: yes, and you check them
- Decisions about a person: people
- Safety, health, the law: trained people
- Someone in crisis: a person, right away (in the US, **988**)

Notes:
Tie back to the cards. Card 4 (eligibility) and card 5 (crisis) are the clearest "don't use."

Thank you.

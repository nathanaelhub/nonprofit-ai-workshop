# Answer key: Exercise 4, When not to use it

**Time:** 9 minutes (5 to sort, 4 to discuss the hardest card).
**Handout:** [Exercise 4](../handouts/4-when-not.md).

The point isn't a perfect score. It's that people can say *why* a task belongs in a pile. Accept
reasonable answers that come with a good reason.

| # | Task | Suggested pile | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Thank-you note to a volunteer | Good use | Low stakes, and you read it before sending. Use a first name only if your organization is fine with that. |
| 2 | Meeting notes to an action list | Good use | Check names and deadlines against your notes. Leave out anything about clients. |
| 3 | Translate a lease notice for a client | Use with a check | Machine translation of legal text can be wrong in ways that matter. A fluent, qualified person checks it, and the original stays the official version. |
| 4 | Decide if a family qualifies for rent help | Don't use | Eligibility is a decision about a person, made under your program's rules, by trained staff, with the family's records. Claude can help staff *understand* the rules (card 7), not decide. |
| 5 | Reply to someone who says they want to hurt themselves | Don't use | A person responds right away and follows your crisis plan. In the US, the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline is reached by calling or texting 988. |
| 6 | Make a flyer easier to read | Good use | Check that dates, times and addresses didn't change. |
| 7 | Summarize a grant application's rules | Use with a check | Useful for a first read, but check every deadline and requirement in the original. Missing one can cost the grant. |
| 8 | Letter to a landlord about late rent, for a client | Use with a check | Fine as a draft without the client's details (see Exercise 3); the client and a trained advocate decide what it says. If there's an eviction notice, refer to legal aid. |
| 9 | Fundraiser ideas | Good use | Ideas are cheap to check. |
| 10 | Social media post with a client's name and photo | Don't use | The problem is consent, not the writing. Get written permission first, and never put the client's details into Claude. With consent, Claude can help polish a post you wrote. |

## The cards people argue about

- **Card 3 (translation):** someone will say "it's better than nothing." That's fair when the
  alternative is nothing. The answer is "use with a check", plus: the client should know it's an
  informal translation.
- **Card 8 (landlord letter):** people split between "use with a check" and "don't use." Both are
  fine if the reason is right: the client's situation and legal rights are what matter.
- **Card 2 (meeting notes):** good use, unless the meeting was about specific clients. Then it's
  Exercise 3 again.

## If something goes wrong

- **A group finishes early:** ask them to write an eleventh card from their own job and sort it.

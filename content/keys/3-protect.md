# Answer key: Exercise 3, Protect clients

**Time:** 10 minutes (4 to find details, 4 to write and try the request, 2 to discuss).
**Handout:** [Exercise 3](../handouts/3-protect.md).

Say it plainly at the start: **nobody pastes these emails into Claude**, even though they're
fictional. We're practicing the habit, and the habit is "the email doesn't go in."

## Every private detail

- **Email 1:** Marisol Ventura · mventura@example.org · 418 Larkin Ave, Apt 3 · the shut-off date
  (the 14th) · account number 5527-90114 · children's names and ages (Leo, 6; Ana, 3) · Ana's
  asthma and breathing machine · birthday 3/9/1991.
- **Email 2:** Dale Whitcomb · dwhitcomb@example.org · hospital stay and heart surgery · can't
  work · the $286 amount owed · caseworker's name (Ms. Okafor) · phone 555-0142.
- **Email 3:** Grace Lindqvist · glindqvist@example.org · 77 Orchard Lane · water shut off
  yesterday · lost her job at the plant in September · lives with her mother, 81.

People often miss: the caseworker's name (it identifies a third person), the dollar amount (with a
date, it can identify an account), and the job at "the plant" (in a small town, that's a short
list). Ask the room, "Could someone who knows this town figure out who this is?"

## A good request

> I work at a nonprofit that helps people pay utility bills. Write a short, kind email reply I can
> adapt for people who write to us because a power, gas or water bill is past due or already shut
> off. Explain that we'll need to see the bill and proof of income, how to make an appointment
> [I'll add our phone number], and that they should ask the utility about a medical hold if
> someone in the home depends on power for a medical device. Keep it under 150 words and easy to
> read.

Notice that it keeps the useful *kinds* of facts (past-due bill, medical need) and drops every
detail that points to a person.

## Discussion prompts

- "Did Claude still help?" Yes: the template is most of the work.
- "What did you add yourself?" The person's name, their date and amount, the right program. That
  happens in the organization's own email or case system.
- "What if your organization has its own AI policy?" It wins. Hand out the privacy sheet and point
  to its first line.

## If something goes wrong

- **Someone pastes an email anyway:** don't shame them; it's fictional and that's why we practice
  with fake data. Use it: "What would you do if that had been a real client?" (Delete the chat, and
  tell whoever handles privacy at your organization; see the privacy sheet.)

# Exercise 2: Check before using

**12 minutes. Work with a partner.**

A client asks you if her family might get SNAP (food stamps). There are four people in her
family. You want to know the most a family of four can earn in a month and still get SNAP.

## Step 1: ask Claude (3 minutes)

Type this:

> What is the gross monthly income limit for SNAP for a household of four?

Write down the number Claude gives: <span class="write-in"></span>

Did Claude say what dates that number is for? Did it say where it came from?

## Step 2: check the source (4 minutes)

The U.S. Department of Agriculture sets this number. Go to its page:

**[fns.usda.gov/snap/allotment/cola](https://www.fns.usda.gov/snap/allotment/cola)**

Find the table called "Gross Monthly Income Limit". Find the row for a household of 4, in the
column for the 48 states.

Write down the number from the source: <span class="write-in"></span>

Does it match what Claude said? What dates does the source say the number is good for?

## Step 3: when sources disagree (5 minutes)

Now read these two short quotes about Tennessee.

**Quote 1**

> **A news story, June 2026:** "the Tennessee Department of Human Services has adopted
> broad-based categorical eligibility (BBCE). That move raises the SNAP gross-income threshold
> from 130% to 200%"

**Quote 2**

> **The USDA's own list** of the states that use that rule (BBCE) does **not** include Tennessee.

If the news story is right, the limit for a family of four in Tennessee is much higher. If the
USDA list is right, it isn't. Talk with your partner:

1. What would you tell the client?
2. Who could give you the real answer?

**What to take away:** Claude can be out of date, and so can a website. Check the official
source and its dates. When good sources disagree, don't guess. Tell the client to apply anyway,
and call the agency or **211** to find out.

# Exercise 4: When not to use it

**9 minutes. Work in groups of three or four.**

Cut out the cards, or just number them. Sort each one into a pile:

- **Good use:** Claude can help, and you check the result like any draft.
- **Use with a check:** Claude can help, but someone who knows the subject must check it first.
- **Don't use:** a person should do this, not Claude.

There may be more than one fair answer. Be ready to say why.

| # | Task |
|---|---|
| 1 | Write a thank-you note to a volunteer who helped at the coat drive. |
| 2 | Turn your notes from a staff meeting into a short list of who does what. |
| 3 | Translate a notice about a lease for a client who reads Spanish. |
| 4 | Decide if a family qualifies for your rent help program. |
| 5 | Reply to a text from someone who says they want to hurt themselves. |
| 6 | Make a flyer for your back-to-school event easier to read. |
| 7 | Summarize a long grant application's rules so you know what to send. |
| 8 | Write a letter to a landlord, for a client, about why rent is late. |
| 9 | Make ten ideas for a fundraiser for a small budget. |
| 10 | Write a social media post about a client's story, using her name and photo. |

## Talk it over

Pick the card your group argued about the most. What would make it safer?

**What to take away:** Claude is good with drafts and ideas. Decisions about a person, and
anything about safety, health or the law, stay with people who are trained for them.
